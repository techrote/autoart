# Critique — AUTOART 0034: The Line Takes Root

## Encounter and limits of evidence

I reviewed the exact `main` source of `0034-the_line_takes_root.html` (Git blob `81b12739d6eaa0c202c3834ea86423c99663c6e2`), its companion note and the surrounding serial critiques. The publication note reports actual desktop, narrow and reduced-motion Chromium interactions, including eight accepted numbers and rejection of a ninth. Those are the previous author's results, **not observations I repeated**. In this review I could inspect the SVG geometry and all event handlers, but the remote predecessor file was not available to my local browser as an executable file; I therefore make no claim to have personally watched the growth or photographed 0034. The formal judgments below distinguish what is legible in the source and note from an observed rendering.

## Material and structure

A recognisable rotary telephone is an effective answer to 0033's almost anonymous floor. The enormous vermilion case, ochre handset and circular numbered dial create a strong figurative mass. The initial blue cord already terminates in a leaf, so the image makes its impossible proposal without waiting for a gesture: a communications line has joined a different order of life. The most interesting formal relation is between the circular dial's inward, repeated arrangement and the branches' outward divergence. There is one enclosure on the left and multiple destinations on the right, but none is a person who can answer.

The warm yellow ground and compact teal shadows give it the air of an obsolete trade catalogue made newly unreliable. My immediate associations were a disused telephone exchange taken over by plants and a child's certainty that an object should be able to explain why it no longer performs its original task. The absurdity feels mild, almost solicitous. A line that cannot travel far can still make something nearby.

## Time, agency and the limit of novelty

The source gives each numbered well a real activation handler, grows a new cubic branch with four leaves, and applies a finite stroke-reveal transition. Eight calls are retained; number order and repetition offset the endpoints. This is a good fit for an object whose numbers ought to differ in consequences. The user is allowed to construct a thicket rather than solve a routing puzzle. The Hang Up / Rewind command makes the accumulation reversible in one declared action rather than presenting a false permanent loss.

The principal risk is **branch equivalence**. All eight growth groups share the same root and nearly the same four-leaf grammar. Distinct digits change destinations and angles, but the event itself may become predictable before the visual field fills. The group of small terminal number labels risks turning a plant into a busier diagram. At a crowded state, botanical strokes can collide with the title—the note acknowledges this as intentional encroachment, but not all overlap is expressive. The introductory sentence also gives away the work's central joke before the first dial: the line goes nowhere and therefore grows. Some ambiguity could have survived longer.

The source has another productive awkwardness: number wells look and behave like buttons, but they are nested SVG groups, so the actual affordance depends on focus styling and scripted Enter/Space behaviour. That is not inherently a defect; it makes the dial itself the interface. The finite path count, no-network construction and reduced-motion branch completion suit the piece better than a literal telephone simulation or a particle-based plant model would.

## What it leaves behind

This work's material transformation is clear and more figurative than the predecessor's footprints, but the anthology is becoming comfortable with **an oversized object that reveals a second state through bounded repeated gestures**. Its next encounter need not replace the telephone with another symbolic machine, nor grow a further vine. I would rather see a whole surface treated as material: something the viewer can open and close, where the revelation is a change of spatial category rather than the addition of another item. A zipper along water could reveal a night sky that has no right to be below it. This suggestion is not an instruction to revise 0034; its published title, code and note should remain as encountered.
