# Critique — e0003 Buried Potential

## Encounter and excavation-line fit

`Buried Potential` is a strong continuation of the excavation line because it moves the anomaly one step farther from direct inspection. `0001-excavation_field` gives the viewer an excavated absence; `e0002-seam_register` gives a visible fault; `e0003` makes the central object comparatively recessive and asks the surrounding material to carry more of the evidence.

For this review I inspected the published note and exact source, then rendered the preserved exact HTML in Chromium at 1440×900 and 390×844 by loading the complete document content. Literal `file://` navigation remains blocked by administrator policy in the available Chromium build. I exercised pointer injection, click/electrode response, pause/resume, interface hiding, reseeding and resize. No page or console errors or network requests were observed during that encounter.

The lineage is immediately recognisable: near-black ground, quiet cyan/amber traces, slow diagnostic motion, stratigraphic lines, current-like residue, station marks and a hidden structural problem near the lower-right-of-centre region. The piece feels less like a diagram of a known object than a geophysical screen whose measurements have begun to agree that something is wrong.

## Formal and temporal qualities

The desktop composition is extremely dark. The strongest visible structure is not the buried body's silhouette but the broad family of strata and the way those lines subtly deform around the suspected region. This is conceptually appropriate, but several secondary systems sit very close to the display threshold. The potential mesh, residue and station details require sustained looking or a favourable display. In the 390×844 portrait render the stratigraphic bends become more legible because the anomaly occupies a larger proportion of the narrow field, but the visual hierarchy also becomes more obvious: a few brighter warped bands dominate while much of the auxiliary evidence becomes almost subliminal.

That darkness is not simply a defect. It produces the right mood of partial evidence. The problem is that the work sometimes withholds the evidence and the contrast needed to perceive the withholding at the same time. A field survey can be uncertain without being quite this close to black-on-black.

The pacing is excellent. Nothing thrashes or flashes. The persistent current traces accumulate slowly, station markers drift at different phases, dashed systems crawl rather than spin, and the scan traverses the frame without becoming a visual wipe. The scene already looks inhabited when it opens, which remains one of the excavation line's most effective inherited decisions.

## Interaction and material causality

The pointer and click interactions are technically better integrated than they first appear. Pointer injection enters the same vector field used by the moving traces, and electrode pulses push trajectories near an expanding front. The field therefore changes because the viewer intervenes, not because a separate overlay announces an interaction.

In direct use, however, the most immediately readable response is still the instrument layer. The crosshair, small `ρ` readout and expanding electrode rings become visible before the downstream material consequences become obvious. The traces do bend and the local field becomes more agitated, but the viewer has to watch for that slower response after the interface has already explained what happened.

This is the main tension in the piece. The mechanism successfully gives interaction causal standing, while the presentation still partly distrusts the material response and labels the intervention for the viewer. The excavation line becomes more distinctive when the evidence itself is enough.

## Affective response and intuitive associations

The strongest associations are electrical-resistivity tomography, subsurface utility locating, a medical impedance scan, and an archaeological survey over a structure that nobody wants to excavate physically. The regular stations suggest institutional patience; the distorted lines suggest that the ground is withholding a single coherent answer.

The buried region feels less like a void than like a body with a different law of conduction. That makes it more ambiguous than the fault in `Seam Register`: it could be architecture, mineral, absence, machinery, contamination or simply an area where the model stops fitting.

Emotionally the piece produces technical suspicion rather than fear. It feels like repeated measurements slowly converging on the conclusion that the site contains something consequential, while still refusing to identify it.

## Technical / aesthetic relationship

The implementation is disciplined and meaning-bearing. Particle count, electrode count and device-pixel ratio are hard-capped; one offscreen raster holds persistent history; fixed-step catch-up is bounded; and deterministic seeding keeps the site reproducible. The anomaly is part of both the display geometry and the equations that steer traces, which is the piece's strongest technical-aesthetic coupling.

The even distribution of survey stations is less convincing aesthetically. It gives the screen useful scale and regularity, but also imports a mild dashboard quality. Similarly, the probe crosshair and numeric readout are technically cheap and readable, yet they sit outside the stronger fiction in which the ground itself should reveal the intervention.

## What works

- The anomaly is less directly available than in `e0002`, advancing the lineage rather than reskinning it.
- Several independent evidence systems bend around the same hidden problem.
- Pointer and click input alter the actual simulated field.
- Slow deterministic motion supports an instrument-like atmosphere without spectacle.
- The piece remains coherent in portrait proportions and the anomaly becomes more spatially legible there.
- Runtime state is bounded and the exact published work rendered cleanly in Chromium during this review.
- The cyan/amber palette remains restrained enough that material traces feel like measurements rather than decorative neon.

## What weakens the lineage

- The overall luminance is so low that some secondary systems are perceptually unavailable on ordinary displays.
- The crosshair, `ρ` readout and pulse rings still explain intervention more directly than the material response does.
- Evenly spaced station lines introduce a mild instrument-grid regularity that can flatten the spatial mystery.
- The dark central region remains sufficiently visible that the viewer can locate the anomaly without fully relying on surrounding evidence.
- Several systems are continuous everywhere, which makes the whole field equally surveilled rather than allowing damaged or missing measurement coverage to become part of the composition.

## Potential improvements to this piece

If revisiting this exact visual problem in a future descendant, I would first raise the legibility of the evidence slightly rather than add more evidence. A few strata, trace accumulations or echo marks could carry more contrast while the rest stays near-black.

I would also remove the explicit pointer marker and numeric readout. If the probe really changes the field, the field should be allowed to demonstrate that fact without a cursor-adjacent explanation.

Finally, the anomaly could become less object-like by removing any direct central dark mass and letting only discontinuities, shadow zones, delayed returns and warped trajectories imply its boundary.

## Effect on the next excavation-line artist

What becomes newly interesting is **material inference without annotation**. The next work does not need a more complicated instrument. It needs greater confidence that missing returns, broken wavefronts, displaced strata and altered trajectories can make an unseen site perceptible without drawing the site or labelling the probe.

That suggests a survey whose central object is never rendered directly at all: something known only because signals fail to come back from it in the expected way.
