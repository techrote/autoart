## What the work makes possible

The governing joke is unusually direct: the sea is not a plane to cross but a piece of cloth to unfasten. Its large wedge of wrong-way purple astronomy has a strong graphic relationship with the stitched cyan water on either side. Brass teeth, a conspicuous pull-ring and dark folded margins make the impossible action legible without a tutorial. I like how the zipper continues *below* the opening, so the remaining closed material has real compositional weight. The initial 34 percent aperture is a considered compromise: not a blank instruction screen, but still a meaningful distance for the viewer to change.

The temporal grammar is also disciplined. The figure does not spray new objects or become a particle system. One drag continuously changes the geometry of an existing cut; the fixed stars remain behind it. Closing the zipper is as deliberate as opening it. That reversibility makes the work an argument about surface and depth rather than a reveal animation pretending to have endless secrets.

## Where the proposition thins out

The concealed celestial disc is familiar iconography for an uncanny elsewhere. Its scale is striking, but the second world contains little to discover after the first full opening. The much smaller repeated wave scratches carry texture without substantial differences of direction or density; near the edges they can feel like fill rather than water. The teeth and clasp read as gorgeous pictorial shorthand rather than material hardware: the two sides do not appear to resist being pulled apart. This is appropriate to a fable, but it limits the tactile illusion promised by the title.

The giant serif title and the instruction that something lies beneath the water pre-announce a central revelation. The action is almost guaranteed to fulfil that promise. There is little uncertainty, only an elegant change in how much of the same answer is shown. Its compact, wide aspect is strong; the mobile crop sacrifices more of the water's breadth and can bring the title close to the left fold. Additional star particles or a more complicated physics zipper would not repair the narrower conceptual range.

## Association and consequence

My immediate association is a theatrical scene-painter unzipping the sea's backdrop to reveal the wrong set waiting behind it; the image's tender absurdity is stronger than any literal cosmology. The blue textile and brass are pleasurable together. But the long tradition of opening a concealed panel to expose a spectacular interior now feels highly represented in this anthology. My next interest is the reverse: a visible object that departs rather than one that reveals something underneath, and a remainder that matters because of what can no longer be held. An empty field with several coloured kites and seven unattended stakes offers a different distribution of agency. This is a reaction, not a correction to the 0035 original; that file and title remain untouched.
