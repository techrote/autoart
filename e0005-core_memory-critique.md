# Critique — e0005 Core Memory

## Formal / stylistic qualities

`Core Memory` is recognisably part of the excavation lineage: a near-black cross-section, restrained cyan and amber strata, slow procedural residue, sparse survey notation and a hidden site that is inferred through surrounding behaviour. Its strongest formal development is the vertical bore rhythm. The untouched state is deliberately austere, while repeated drilling adds narrow persistent marks that intersect the horizontal strata and make the worked section read more clearly as accumulated evidence rather than a static diagram.

The main formal weakness is luminance. Fine traces remain close to the threshold of visibility, so the untouched state can look flatter than the later worked state. Repeated vertical marks can also become regular enough to suggest a survey worksheet rather than a materially unstable section.

## Temporal and interactive behaviour

The slow bore descent is effective. A click does not place a finished mark immediately; the core progresses from the surface toward a selected depth, which gives interaction duration and consequence. Pointer bias remains temporary and visually subordinate, while bore scars persist for the current seeded section. That division between transient probing and persistent sampling is one of the piece's clearest ideas.

The main limitation is reciprocity. The section records where the viewer has acted, and nearby strata and traces react locally, but the surrounding material does not reorganise very far beyond the bore itself. The viewer supplies most of the decisive change.

## Atmosphere and associations

The piece suggests a geological core log, an archaeological section and a diagnostic instrument at the same time. The pre-existing scars make the site feel previously investigated before the viewer arrives. The mood is patient and technical rather than spectacular. Its most distinctive atmospheric effect comes from the fact that the section becomes more legible as evidence accumulates.

## Technical / aesthetic tension

The implementation supports the concept well. Live traces, bore records, device-pixel ratio and fixed-step catch-up are bounded, while persistent marks live in a separate raster history. The hidden anomaly participates in trace motion, bore deflection and sample continuity rather than existing only as a visual mask.

A conceptual mismatch appears when the live bore cap is exceeded: an old raster scar can remain visible after its detailed bore record has left the active list, so visual history can outlive dynamic influence. The piece therefore remembers more graphically than physically.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths include slow consequential interaction, a coherent dark survey language, a hidden anomaly that remains discoverable through material effects, and a convincing cross-sectional rhythm after several interventions. Weaknesses include the very dim startup state, increasing regularity under repeated drilling, and a stronger sense of recording than of structural response.

## Excavation-line contribution and next direction

`Core Memory` successfully extends `0001-excavation_field` by giving interaction persistent consequence without turning the work into a conventional editor. It also preserves the e-series move toward indirect evidence: the site is learned through how cores bend, disappear or alter the surrounding field.

The most interesting next problem is **material reciprocity**. A subsequent excavation-line piece could let a cut or cavity change the wider section over time through settlement, redistributed load, secondary discontinuities or other bounded structural response. The viewer should remain able to identify their intervention, but the site's later form should no longer be determined only by that intervention.
