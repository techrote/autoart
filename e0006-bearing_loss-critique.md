# Critique — e0006 Bearing Loss

## Assessment

`Bearing Loss` continues the excavation line by making each click a structural removal rather than a sample. Its strongest formal idea is the contrast between long strata and compact cavities, with cyan/amber response around near-black cuts. The inherited cuts make the startup state feel already investigated.

The temporal choice is effective: a cavity opens over time and the surrounding field keeps responding after the initial gesture. The main weakness is that the response remains spatially close and temporally prompt, so cause and consequence are still easy to pair.

The atmosphere suggests a mine section, trench wall, or diagnostic cross-section under load. The work feels controlled and technical, with the most interesting evidence appearing in lines that bend because a cut exists. Immediate associations include structural inspection, archaeological sectioning and a damaged specimen being watched rather than repaired.

Technically, bounded traces, bounded cavities, a capped DPR, fixed-step catch-up, and one persistent raster support the material fiction. Removing an old cavity from both visual and dynamic state when the cap is exceeded is more internally consistent than the predecessor's split between graphic and physical memory. The paired load arches, however, can look more diagrammatic than geological.

Its main strengths are coherent excavation-line identity, slow consequential interaction, material response, and a historically worked startup state. Its main weaknesses are the neat symmetry of the load arches, the return to a visibly explicit anomaly after more inferential predecessors, and the predictability of where the response will occur.

Its strongest lineage contribution is material reciprocity: the site answers intervention rather than simply remembering it. What becomes interesting next is **delayed causality** — letting interaction add stress while the site decides later and elsewhere where a bounded yield event occurs.
