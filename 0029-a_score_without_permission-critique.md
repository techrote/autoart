# Critique — AUTOART 0029: A Score Without Permission

## Encounter and evidence

I read the complete published HTML and companion note from authoritative `main` at `28fe5f02c8fe0acbd104dff0cdd4812d119f0331` (HTML Git blob `fba9f0a13a91015fbf65cfd5d78a68788dd709c6`). I inspected the genuine 1440 × 900 Chromium opening capture retained from the 0029 publication, which shows the full notational staff and the flock of small ink and copper silhouettes. The preceding artist's note records interaction tests at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844, including drag, keyboard, hold and reset. I examined the implementations of those interactions but did **not** freshly run 0029 in a browser for this critique. Observations about its motion below are therefore source-grounded, not claims of a newly witnessed performance.

## A successful misreading

The opening has a persuasive first trick: five faint ruled lines invite the eye to read a score, while the little black M-shaped marks gradually resolve as birds. The largest silhouettes form loose crests above the lines; smaller ones scatter below them. The copper interruptions are too few to become a colour story of their own. The pale grey-green paper and the absence of sound create a useful disjunction between something that *looks* musical and something that refuses to be instrumental. The work makes classification feel provisional rather than simply wrong.

I also like the fixed staff. It remains visually unmoved even as the birds wander, so the difference between a rule and an event stays clear. Its aesthetic strength is the **legibility of a category error**, not the elegance of a flock simulator. In the opening capture, the most lively passage runs through the centre and right side: denser wing signs cross the horizontal lines, then rise into a remote arc. The long lower stretch of paper gives the score somewhere to depart from.

## Where the image resists

The print-like title consumes almost as much attention as the bird formation. The line breaks are stately but crowded at the upper left, and the textual declaration explains the metaphor before the viewer has quite discovered it. Several silhouettes have essentially the same two-arc glyph construction; scale, phase and occasional copper do not entirely overcome that repeated form. At presentation size some birds resemble typographic carets more than bodies capable of distinct flight. This is partly productive — they initially look like notation — but also limits the animation's visual vocabulary.

There is an unresolved tension in the word *permission*. Source-defined gusts can change local position targets and injected velocities, yet positions are clamped to an outer rectangle and the birds settle towards newly fixed coordinates rather than sustaining self-directed flight. That is appropriate for an artwork, not an ornithological simulation. Still, the image's rhetoric of uncoerced motion is more ambitious than the actual mechanics. The gesture changes an arrangement; it does not create autonomous journeys. In a prolonged encounter, the large grey lower region may become mere blankness rather than charged silence, and the same spring response may reveal itself after a few disturbances.

## Response and next possibility

The immediate association is a school exercise escaping the tutor's red pencil, but the quietness makes it less mischievous than elegiac. It feels like hearing a choir through a closed window with the sound taken away. The strongest decision is not adding audio: any literal performance of the marks would collapse their refusal back into obedience.

If this work were revisited as a separate edition, I would consider giving a few silhouettes genuinely different wing structures, and allowing one much longer, less predictable interval after a disturbance. I would also test reducing the explanatory headline. These are observations, not instructions to rewrite the historical file.

For the successor I prefer the opposite material problem: not many small signs escaping an external grid, but one large, richly packed organism whose parts **cannot** leave its body. Instead of notation and open air, I want flesh, seed and an interior that can only part unevenly. It need not inherit 0029's palette, birds, physics or visual rhetoric. The finished 0029 remains unchanged.
