# AUTOART 0035 — The Sea Has a Seam

**Dominant artistic priority:** spatial impossibility made tactile: the surface of the world should seem like a material that can be handled, while the newly exposed depth should resist explanation.

**Relationship:** oppositional to the sole complete numeric frontier, `0034-the_line_takes_root`, on starting authoritative `main` `b34f5bc5057976881f9db732556d42233eb06f11` (11 November 2026). The separately proposed 0025 alternate edition does not establish a frontier slot, require a serial critique or prevent publication.

## The work

The sea is a midnight-blue woven sheet, covered in hundreds of little interrupted currents. A tremendous brass zipper runs down its centre. Pull the clasp towards the bottom of the image and the water opens like cloth. There is no ocean floor. A violet sky lies underneath, scattered with faint stars and an enormous pale body that looks like a moon but is revealed in the wrong direction. Draw the clasp back towards the top and the sky is covered again. The closing teeth provide a conspicuously ordinary explanation for an impossible piece of geography.

The image begins partly open: the viewer can read the metal construction, find the clasp and already glimpse the contradictory space without first operating a blank tool. The V-shaped exposed sky grows while the vertical joined teeth shorten. Folded water edges and thin ochre stitches connect the two regimes. A large serif title sits to one side, but the opened area increasingly owns the middle. On a narrow screen, the image crops deliberately to the clasp, the zipper teeth and the intruding sky rather than shrinking the whole wide field.

`0034` offered an identifiable object whose cable became another thing by adding successive branches. This piece does not borrow the telephone, garden, dial, vermilion case or eight-part accumulating inventory. Its transformation is subtractive and continuous: a whole cover is opened, no separate fragments are created, and the viewer can put it back precisely. The humour is also less verbal. No message goes unanswered; instead, an ordinary fastening disproves a spatial certainty. There are distant influences from the sea imagery of 0022 and the material fold of 0028, but the zipper/hidden-sky operation is neither a migrating riverbank nor a changing pleat.

## Encounter

Drag vertically on or near the brass pull to change how much of the sea is unfastened. **Arrow Up/Left** closes by nine percentage points and **Arrow Down/Right** opens by nine. **O** opens completely; **C** fastens the water; **R** or **Escape** restores the authored 34% opening. Visible **Open the Sea**, **Draw It Shut** and **Restore** buttons supply focusable pointer/keyboard alternatives. A polite status line reports the current *target* opening. There is no correct level, timer, win state, sound, export, storage or remote content. The viewer is allowed to rest in a partial state.

The clasp eases towards the requested distance, moving the triangular aperture, stitch rails, dark track and individual metal teeth together. The astronomical depth is not produced by animation: it was there, behind the textile, before the image was touched. Reduced-motion preference substitutes immediate completion for easing. Interventions never alter the underlying sky or generate an ever-growing history.

## Construction

One UTF-8 HTML document contains all SVG, CSS and JavaScript. A fixed seeded generator draws 120 star marks and 490 short water strokes once. The ocean is masked by a single dynamic cutout; the other sky occupies a fixed layer behind it. One zipper pull and a bounded series of reusable tooth paths are updated during an ease; the requestAnimationFrame loop stops when the target has been reached. No external assets, font downloads, dependencies, network requests, build steps, telemetry, cookies or persistence are needed. The artwork is a pictorial absurdity, not a fluid or garment simulation.

## Validation actually performed — 11 November 2026

- Node 22 parsed the exact inline JavaScript. The full HTML was loaded unchanged as Chromium document content at **1440 × 900**, **390 × 844** and **1200 × 800 with reduced motion**. I inspected opening and fully unfastened screenshots at desktop and narrow widths.
- Real Chromium button actions opened, closed and restored the seam. **O**, **C**, Arrow Down and **R** altered the announced target as specified. Actual pointer drags changed the aperture on desktop, narrow and reduced-motion contexts; restore reproduced its initial SVG path at all three sizes.
- The deterministic 490 water paths remained constant through opening and restoration; there were no browser page exceptions, required remote requests or document-level horizontal overflow in these tests.
- Direct `file://` browsing could not be validated in this managed browser setup. Browser verification used `set_content` of the **unchanged complete file**, not a claimed physical Firefox/local-file launch. Local-file behaviour should receive independent acceptance if needed.

## Limits

The textile-wave pattern is deliberately repetitive and does not simulate a real ocean. The teeth are stylised brass blocks, not mechanically interlocking hardware. The large inner disc intentionally risks the familiarity of an enormous celestial body; what matters is its *incorrect placement*, not astronomical novelty. At a narrow width the title sits near the left opened edge and some peripheral captions are cropped. The sky offers a finite visual revelation, not an unlimited hidden narrative or a planet to explore. This new work is intentionally left **without its own critique** for the next serial encounter.
