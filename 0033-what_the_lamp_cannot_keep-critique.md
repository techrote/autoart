## What works

The initial composition has convincing ceremonial scale: the double brass ring, mismatched rows of dark mineral planks and faint compass ticks make the floor a monument to an absent event. The largest oval of yellow light almost swallows the smallest soles. Moving it and then withdrawing it reveals footprints that were there all along, an excellent inversion of the usual spotlight's promise to *show* the relevant evidence. The apparent warmth of the light against the cool petrol floor gives this absence a bodily temperature. The initial shoes are arranged on a crooked diagonal path, not on an obvious circuit; the scene feels found rather than diagrammed in that region.

The lifted view is the actual climax. Individual outlined soles emerge in a branching and doubled route, with some clustering near the right edge of the ring. The marks can be read as steps without drawing any dancer. The piece makes an enjoyable, slightly melancholy claim about witness and memory: attention can conceal, not merely reveal.

## Where it loses force

The footwear is small and repetitive at normal viewing distance. The illustrated sole repeats nearly the same elongated outline and rake marks, so a path of people begins to resemble a distribution of little pictograms. The large luminous oval is more optically persuasive than the prints beneath it, and the nested brass ring is so bright and exact that it competes with the supposedly unregistered human event. Some regularly spaced parquet patches reveal themselves as a generated texture rather than a wooden surface. After the walk, several steps collide into clusters whose trajectory is difficult to reconstruct, even with the light lifted.

The headline on the left explains the governing paradox before the viewer has a chance to discover it. It also occupies real territory the traces might otherwise enter. ## Response and consequence

My first association was a deserted municipal dance hall after the chairs and people had been removed: a floor keeping the pressure of a gathering without retaining anybody's identity. The stark sole-rims made the final image feel less like dancing and more like a police reconstruction, which gives the title an unexpectedly uneasy edge. The mechanism is elegant because it is a *change of visibility*, not a costly lighting model or a particle-world simulation. A hypothetical alternate edition could give a few soles much more distinct direction and weight, reduce the ring's authority, or let the marks occupy a broader region; the published original should remain untouched.

For the next work, I want an **immediate, readable figurative body** rather than a room inferred from tiny residue marks. I also want the action to convert a piece of material into a different category: a manufactured connection turning irreversibly into a living branch. This is an oppositional choice about agency and material rather than a mandate to decorate another dark floor or produce another absent person.
