## Work

A giant moth holds two incompatible fictional maps in its wings. The left pair consists of ochre, coral, plum and circular sediment-like contours; the right is cold mineral green-blue with angular directional markings. The segmented body is small compared with its two territories. Neither map names a country. Both are printed on the same specimen without any gesture towards determining which is true.

The authored opening is already a complete pictorial plate: big organic wing lobes against dark teal, hand-cut contours, eye marks and sparse archival ticks. The viewer can fold either wing independently, or drag it to intermediate spreads. **Take Print** adds a translucent outline of the *current arrangement* underneath the creature; at most eight prints remain, making an imperfect record of earlier readings. Restore clears only the added rubbings and returns the initial wings. The contradictory maps are never reconciled.

The predecessor used a red line and precise dark architectural teeth to make absence legible through local coupling. The moth instead occupies the image with an asymmetrical, chromatically excessive naturalist body. A gesture changes how a surface can be seen and leaves a second-order trace; it does not force adjacent mechanisms to compensate. Earlier tactile cut-paper works, especially 0011, are a remote influence, not its subject or a source of assets.

## Controls and technique

Click or tap either wing to fold/unfold. Drag horizontally over a wing to set its spread continuously. **1** and **2** toggle the respective wings; **P** or **Take Print** makes one impression; **R** or **Restore** removes impressions and restores the initial pose. Each wing group is independently focusable and accepts Enter/Space. Buttons are focusable, and a live textual readout reports both spreads and print count.

This is a single offline HTML document, with inline SVG, CSS and JavaScript. Four fixed organic paths are reused for the main wings and their bounded printed outlines. Decorative contour families and paper marks are deterministic, index-generated, not actual cartography. A single animation loop eases only while a wing is moving, and reduced-motion preference applies transformations immediately. There are no external images, fonts, downloads, accounts, cookies, telemetry, audio, network requests or persistent state.
