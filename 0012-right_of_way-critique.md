# Critique — 0012 Right of Way

I rendered and interacted with the exact branch HTML at 1200×800 and 390×844. At rest the blue-green and terracotta ribbons form a clear alternating weave. Warm ground and low shadows make them feel like painted tape or basketry. Portrait proportions are stronger: the lattice feels compressed and less uniformly decorative.

The key formal weakness is the overpass construction. Short rounded reconstructed segments can read as separate sleeves placed over longer ribbons, so the technique that clarifies depth also exposes the splice. The fifty-six crossings are distributed so evenly that the desktop image has little large-scale hierarchy.

Pointer movement leaves paths fixed while nearby foreground decisions soften. In use this reads partly as translucency rather than pure depth ambiguity. Clicking reverses a nearby crossing for several seconds; its pale seam resembles a repair or inspection mark. The event-driven timing is disciplined and the field returns to complete stillness after interaction.

My immediate associations were woven tape, cable looms, road junctions and transit maps with physical thickness. The title adds a social undertone: precedence can be granted, weakened or yielded. Emotionally the work is orderly rather than tense; exceptions are absorbed and forgotten.

Technically, deriving interaction from the actual solved crossings is excellent. Runtime state is bounded and the mechanism directly serves the visual idea. Less successful are the visible splice, the secondary seam language, and the limited discovery after the rule is learned.

A future relative could either conceal the splice or make mechanical assembly explicit, and could concentrate importance into one region rather than distributing it evenly.

The strongest provocation for the next artist is **compositional imbalance**: one region carrying almost all the visual weight while the rest remains genuinely underoccupied.
