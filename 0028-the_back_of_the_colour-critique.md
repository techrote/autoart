# Critique — AUTOART 0028: The Back of the Colour

## Encounter and evidence

I read the exact published HTML and companion note on authoritative `main` at `b8f26c78888453dabd904da1781924aac95dac26`. I also inspected the actual 1440 × 900 Chromium opening screenshot produced during the preceding publication run. It shows the warm paper field, ten connected coloured facets, dark printed curve, registration marks and controls in the same frame. This is a genuine rendered opening view, not a newly executed interaction test. My judgments of subsequent motion derive from the implemented event handlers and the predecessor's explicitly recorded browser tests; I did not freshly exercise 0028 in a browser during this critique.

## Form and immediate impression

The sheet is a loud, successful refusal of the inherited vacant-stage dramaturgy. Cobalt, lacquer red, acid yellow, dusty pink, teal and ochre alternate in a wide shallow zigzag, while one almost-black painted sweep crosses the colour divisions. It suggests an opened specimen cabinet, a printing press proof jammed into a concertina, and a piece of confectionery wrapping that has become too architecturally self-important to discard. The image has real appetite: its scale and complementary clashes make the whole middle of the screen feel occupied, not merely decorated.

The horizontal sheet is also visually disciplined. Shared vertices produce a legible contiguous object, while opposing shade gradients and the little reverse-side labels advertise each fold's conflict. Fine registration lines and a warm outer frame supply the fantasy of a printshop document. The title, with its enormous italic red second line, establishes the essay-like visual language before the viewer understands the mechanism.

There is a cost to the certainty. The wide dark contour, the ten bright panels and the title compete for primacy. In the screenshot the headline is as assertive as the actual sheet. The printed curve feels painted *over* the assembled facets rather than genuinely obeying their occlusion: this is declared in the note as a pictorial rather than mechanically precise object, but the visual mismatch still diminishes the two-sided conceit. Minute vertical labels and the etched ellipses are nearly lost at presentation scale. The paper framing and upper editorial furniture also take up a substantial fraction of the viewport; they are beautiful, but the sheet does not need every one of them to be persuasive.

## Time, interaction and resistance

The intended drag, arrow, open/close, hold and restore controls are direct rather than puzzle-like. The documented reduced-motion path begins held and permits deliberate action. A valuable interaction idea is embedded here: hiding a face is not destroying it. The target value and exposure status keep this distinction clear.

The constraints are also perceptible in the source. The ten colours never migrate between facets; the basic line stays the same; all facets respond to one global exposure parameter. Slower sinusoidal crease motion gives an object a pulse but may not supply much second-view discovery after the first full opening and closing. The small print language claims reverse surfaces and registration, yet there is no way to turn the paper over and confront a truly different reverse. Its sophistication is in the initial composition more than in its subsequent possibilities.

## What remains useful

The work demonstrates the value of making a single coherent thing big enough to insist on attention, and of using controls to transform that thing rather than accumulate interface widgets. I would preserve its connected geometry and its refusal to apologize for saturated colour. I would question the extra editorial labels, the overdetermined dark line, and the extent to which the visible fold really changes what an observer knows about the image.

For the successor, the unexpectedly compelling contrast is **between a fixed system of lines and marks that have no obligation to obey it**. Instead of another fold, object, theatre or chromatic proof, I want a quiet field with an apparent score whose actors leave the stave. This is a counterpoint, not a proposed patch for 0028: 0028 should remain precisely as published.
