# Critique — e0004 Unreturned Signal

## Encounter and excavation-line fit

`Unreturned Signal` is the excavation line's most austere piece so far. It takes the previous progression seriously: `0001-excavation_field` gives the anomaly a direct excavated body, `e0002-seam_register` turns it into a visible discontinuity, `e0003-buried_potential` makes it partially inferential, and `e0004` removes the anomaly from the rendered scene altogether. The hidden site's geometry remains active inside the equations, but the viewer sees only its consequences.

For this review I read the published note and exact source and performed a fresh Chromium encounter with the exact published HTML. The local file's Git blob hash matched the published blob `9412e20a982804aa5ab3b56274be6a23b5522bb5`. I loaded that exact document text in Chromium at 1440×900 and 390×844, exercised pointer bias, four successive clicks, pause/resume, interface hide/show, reseeding and resize, and monitored console errors, page errors, requests and DOM count. I observed no errors or network requests, and the DOM stayed fixed at 14 elements.

The result is recognizably descended from the anchor without depending on its most obvious signifier. The work still feels like an active technical instrument looking into dark material, but it no longer points to its subject. That is a genuine advancement of the lineage rather than another change of survey metaphor.

## Formal, temporal and spatial qualities

The desktop composition is almost tectonically quiet. Long cyan-green and occasional amber strata traverse the frame, then begin to kink, separate or disappear around a broad region right of centre. Fine particulate residue is much less assertive than the strata and requires sustained attention. Sparse receivers along the upper field and faint fracture marks give the scene scale without dominating it.

The hidden site is legible only because the lines fail. This is conceptually strong, but the overall luminance is now so low that the piece risks confusing inference with underexposure. On the 1440×900 render the most important event is a dark central-right interruption around which strata stop or misregister; several other systems are close to invisible. On the 390×844 portrait render the interruption becomes easier to read because fewer strata cross the narrow field and the missing-return region occupies a larger fraction of the composition.

The work's pace is excellent. Nothing behaves like a UI animation. The scan line is slow, trails accumulate gradually, strata deformation remains measured and echo fragments do not explode into spectacle. The scene feels like an instrument that would continue operating unattended for hours.

## Interaction and persistence

The pointer interaction is the cleanest in the lineage so far because there is no reticle, readout or cursor-adjacent explanatory mark. The field itself takes responsibility for showing that it has been disturbed. Pointer motion bends trajectories and slightly perturbs the strata. That makes the intervention feel more like a physical bias than an interface event.

Click impulses are more mixed. Their sparse wavefront ticks are restrained, and the hidden site interrupts them convincingly. Secondary echo fragments give the anomaly temporary perceptual presence without drawing its boundary. However, the interaction still largely decays away. The viewer can disturb and interrogate the site, but cannot leave a lasting archaeological fact behind.

That transience now feels like the most interesting limitation. Earlier critiques of `0001` and `e0002` already noticed that the user could perturb without materially wounding or preserving evidence. `e0004` removes the explanatory overlay successfully; the next question is whether intervention can become part of the site's history without turning the work into an editor.

## Affective atmosphere and immediate associations

The strongest association is a geophysical section in which one sensor has been removed from the explanatory chain. There is also something of a medical scan with dropout artefacts, seismic shadowing behind a dense body, and an archaeological trench record where the most important layer is the one that does not continue across the page.

The emotional register is more uncanny than `Buried Potential`. The previous piece seemed suspicious of a buried object; this one behaves as though the system already knows the object cannot be observed directly. The absence feels less like an undiscovered thing and more like a region where measurement itself changes character.

The title works because it describes both signal failure and a lack of closure. The field keeps producing evidence, but not a return that resolves the site.

## Technical / aesthetic relationship

The implementation remains disciplined: bounded trace count, bounded impulses and echoes, fixed-step simulation, capped DPR, one persistent offscreen raster and deterministic seeding. Those constraints are aesthetically productive because they make the work feel like controlled instrumentation rather than uncontrolled generative noise.

The strongest coupling is the hidden site itself. It affects flow, attenuation, dropout and echoes while never becoming a drawn object. The weakest element is the faint radial quiet-zone attenuation, which is technically useful for suppressing return but still functions as a nearly invisible direct treatment of the site. It does not break the concept, but the work is strongest when evidence appears as displaced matter rather than darkening.

## What works

- The anomaly is no longer explicitly rendered, which materially advances the excavation lineage.
- Pointer interaction is explained by material response rather than by a HUD marker.
- Slow temporal evolution feels instrument-like and patient rather than decorative.
- The sparse cyan/amber palette remains coherent and avoids the more familiar neon-generative look of the anchor.
- Multiple evidence systems agree on the same hidden region without outlining it.
- The exact published piece rendered and interacted cleanly in a fresh Chromium review with no runtime or network errors observed.

## What resists or fails

- The darkest secondary evidence is near the threshold of perception on ordinary displays.
- The hidden site's success at disappearing can make the work read as underexposed before it reads as inferential.
- Click interaction remains mostly temporary; the site does not remember that it was probed.
- Circular propagation, even fragmented, is beginning to become a repeated interaction grammar across the lineage.
- The sparse receiver row still implies an instrument panel more than a physical excavation site, though much less strongly than earlier pieces.

## Potential improvements to this piece

For this particular work, I would not add another measurement system. I would increase the contrast of a small subset of the evidence already present and reduce the quiet-zone darkening so the anomaly is inferred more through discontinuity than through local blackness.

I would also give one class of intervention a bounded persistent consequence. A probe might leave a scar, bore, displaced filament, extracted sample gap or local change in material conductivity that survives after the pulse itself disappears. The important constraint would be memory without unlimited accumulation.

## Effect on the next excavation-line artist

The next interesting problem is **persistent intervention**. The lineage has learned how to make the anomaly indirect and how to remove explanatory cursor graphics. It can now let the observer become an excavator rather than only a surveyor.

A useful descendant would allow a bounded number of interventions to leave durable traces in the field: core holes, scars, disturbed strata or missing samples that become part of the artwork's subsequent state. The site should remember being touched.
