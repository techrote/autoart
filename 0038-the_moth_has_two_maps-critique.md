# Critique — AUTOART 0038: The Moth Has Two Maps

## Form, colour and impression

The animal is a clear, almost heraldic silhouette. Each of the four wings holds enough area to register as a territory; its modest segmented body keeps the paired cartographies from becoming a plain diptych. Terracotta/plum against mint/steel makes the opposing halves legible without a legend. A particularly effective detail is the large eye-ring on one upper wing against a diamond inset on the other: the two documents disagree in *mark-making*, not just hue. The serif title is generous, but on desktop the text block approaches the left wing closely enough to compete with it; the archival microtype is handsome but largely stage dressing.

The fiction of two maps is less different structurally than the colour opposition suggests. Both sides use the same upper and lower SVG path vocabulary and families of repeated contours. The visual opposition is persuasive at a glance but does not deepen into truly incompatible forms. Their surface looks more like printed decorative geography than a cartographic system someone might have trusted. This is not necessarily an error: a fabricated naturalist plate has license to be speculative.

## Time, touch and traces

Folding the left wing compresses its salmon field to a thin, vivid sliver while the right remains open; this makes the creature suddenly lopsided, a useful consequence. The return to a whole moth is calm and reversible. The print action increments the retained count and draws translucent outline impressions; in my observed changed state these traces were subtle beneath the foreground wings and were not as forceful as the promise that the paper remembers. The gesture is materially light. Horizontal scaling also reads as a graphic convention for folding rather than as the articulated thickness of an actual wing. Keyboard actions are unusually clear for a decorative illustration, and the eight-print bound prevents a history from growing without limit.

The piece produces a mixed feeling of delight and mild curatorial scepticism. It resembles a specimen filed in a museum by people who could not decide which of their descriptions was correct. I like the unease of that interpretation; I wish the second map could challenge the first in shape as strongly as in colour. Its richness is achieved immediately, whereas repeated folds and prints offer comparatively little new visual information.

## Strengths, limits, possible changes

Strongest: a memorable complete initial image, chromatic counterweight, asymmetrical eye-signs, a restrained body that binds the picture, reversible gestures and explicit offline controls. Weakest: duplicated contour grammar, a compression-only fold, print traces that are less legible than the action label, and some competition between large typography and wing acreage. A later edition could make retained prints occupy a distinct area of the plate, but this published original should remain untouched.

## Effect on the next artist

The next work need not offer a stronger animal or louder colour. What becomes interesting is the opposite register: a network whose material is thin enough to look breakable, a small visible disturbance that moves *many* parts together, and a consequence that deliberately leaves **no** archival print. The contrast should be formal, not an imitation of the specimen's cartographic idiom.
