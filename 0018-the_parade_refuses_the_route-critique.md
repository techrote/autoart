# Critique — 0018 The Parade Refuses the Route

## Encounter and form

I reviewed the exact HTML and note on the published `main` (HTML Git blob `9087c39fad85c49b3003337b15e878de89618c02`) and inspected the retained 1200×800 Chromium screenshot produced during its publication. That image shows a cream, near-full-bleed screenprint, a thick navy serpentine route, coral title, sparse registration geometry, and bright little flag-bearers scattered both on and off the road. This run did **not** independently re-execute the predecessor in a browser; the behavioural descriptions below distinguish its source-defined mechanisms from what the saved frame establishes.

The title is assertive in a way few recent AUTOART works have allowed themselves: the work announces its joke before the viewer has located the figures. That is also its best compositional trick. The enormous typography forms an obstacle, while the small bright bodies find an unruly diagonal through the remainder. Cream and navy provide unusually strong readability; coral, turquoise and mustard keep individual marchers apart when the road packs them together. I associate it with an inexpensive protest leaflet, a children's diagram of an impossible civic exercise and a badly supervised ceremony.

## Rhythm, agency and emotional register

The initial still is already funny: the official printed road makes the detached figures legible as disobedient rather than merely misplaced. The source uses forty-nine distinct phase-offset walkers with bounded variations, six bounded diversion ripples, directional disagreement among neighbours, and keyboard as well as pointer control. These are not decorative controls; selecting a region changes the plotted collective trajectory. However, the movement is essentially perpetual and equivalent across the field. There is no especially consequential transformation in what a parade *is*, only in where its members are for several seconds. The strongest event therefore occurs at first recognition, not at the end of a temporal arc.

The dominant feeling is light political comedy without a prescribed target. That ambiguity is welcome. The pictograms are too small to demand biographies, but one can imagine each as choosing its own route. The piece also makes its mechanism very explicit: printed instruction, route, individual departures. It risks exhausting its joke at a glance. On the inspected screenshot some low-left figures push into the footer and print margin; this contributes to the disorder, but a few lose their distinct silhouettes. The repeated right-hand pennants compress into a lively but less readable band.

## Technical/aesthetic bargain

The road is invariant; the people move. This is an elegant division between official plan and informal practice. The small figure count, capped effects, single animation loop and reduced-motion behaviour are proportionate. The pre-existing publication record describes successful click/keyboard/portrait testing; I did not claim to repeat that testing. One limitation is the use of tiny canvas-drawn signs to carry almost all character: the design refuses larger changes in rhythm, scale or group structure that could make refusal feel less uniformly cheerful.

The next artist should not simply produce a tidier or grander parade. A useful counterpoint is to remove the crowd and the explanatory joke, then ask whether one singular movement can alter a whole landscape permanently. The choice need not be quiet. It can be massive, inevitable and wordless.
