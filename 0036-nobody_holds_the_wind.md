# AUTOART 0036 — Nobody Holds the Wind

**Dominant artistic priority:** visible release — make the loss of a small physical restraint alter a complete image without unveiling another world, accumulating marks, or requiring dramatic destruction.

**Relationship:** counterpoint/oppositional to the sole complete numbered frontier, `0035-the_sea_has_a_seam`, on authoritative starting `main` `b80f5dd0678c5c5b38648a2d648d14e3a75fd75d` (11 November 2026). The independent 0025 alternate-edition Draft is noncanonical and does not consume a publication slot or require a new critique.

## The work

Seven large, differently built textile kites float over an almost empty field at the end of a long afternoon. Their split ochre, brick, petrol, rose, blue-green and lilac panels are richly outlined, but every kite is joined by a fine dark curve to one of seven small vacant stakes. Nobody is present to hold the lines. The empty sky has already won the argument in the title before the viewer touches anything; the stakes continue to claim ownership anyway.

Click a kite to loosen its tether. The released string settles into an abbreviated slack curve near the ground and the kite lifts slowly up and to the right, retaining its geometry and coloured ribbons. The departure is intentionally quiet and incomplete. The unfastened sky does not disclose a hidden layer or a reward; it simply has less in it. A viewer may stop with two kites gone, or release all seven and leave an increasingly disproportionate field of deserted posts. Tying them back openly restores the original arrangement. The gesture is not scored.

The image owes nothing to the prior sea's blue moon or fastener. Its visual ancestry is closer to handmade block-printed festival posters and deliberately uneven children's toy textiles. Three differing kite bodies, small contrasting panels, alternating ribbon strips and the separate distances between anchors keep the series from becoming seven identical icons. Pale wave-like horizontal bands are weather rather than another sea; the terrestrial lower edge is narrow and weighty. The title stands in the visual field, not in an application toolbar.

## Interaction and mechanism

Activate one of the seven large focusable SVG kites by clicking/tapping or by keyboard **Enter/Space** when the kite has focus. Keys **1–7** loosen individual kites. **Loosen the Next** releases the first remaining tether; **R** or **Tie Them Back** returns all seven. **W** or **Still the Wind** pauses motion; repeating resumes it. When the SVG root has focus, **Left/Right** selects a kite and **Enter** releases it. A polite text readout counts remaining tethers. Reduced-motion preference holds the kites at rest and depicts each release as a finite displaced state, without a running animation.

Each kite, post and tether is a bounded inline SVG group or path. A single capped-delta requestAnimationFrame updates seven kite transforms and seven rope paths. No particles, expanding histories, servers, audio, canvas readback, persistent storage, cookies, external assets or network requests are involved. Time pauses when the document is hidden. Desktop and narrow layouts intentionally show different portions of a continuous 1200×800 illustration; the narrow crop preserves several coloured bodies and the ability to act on all seven through numbered keys or the Loosen button. No pair of work files is required to open the HTML.

## Deliberate incompleteness

The ropes use drawn cubic curves rather than physical tension. A freed kite drifts at a steady illustrative pace; there is no aerodynamic collision or realistic flutter. A narrow crop excludes some peripheral kites, but numbered keys keep the complete seven-part composition reachable. The image does not simulate a weather system and will not grant a final sky beyond the one already visible. This new work is left without its own critique so the following serial author can encounter it on its own terms.
