## What the image does

The opening is a carefully staged collection rather than an abstract effect: five differently proportioned architectural bays, bone-coloured artifacts, shallow plinths, beams of sepia light and enormous black cut-paper silhouettes. The silhouetted fish above an urn and tree above a chair are instantly legible contradictions. The palette of aubergine, brass, ochre and ceramic rose is controlled, and the small catalog labels set a bureaucratic temperature without resorting to an interface panel. I associate it with a fraudulent ethnographic museum, or evidence boxes whose labels were shuffled during an overnight relocation.

The contrast between intact objects and displaced shadows is both concept and mechanism. When activated, the silhouettes pass one another on different elevated paths, making the exchange spatially readable. The screenshot taken mid-flight shows the bird and key no longer at their original bays while the objects are unaltered. That is a clearer, more imaginative temporal event than simply changing colour or instantly exchanging IDs.

## Resistance

The work also explains its riddle very quickly. Fish, tree and key are so specific that the initial proposition is resolved almost before the viewer has explored the five exhibits. Because the choreography and two-neighbour exchange remain the same, repeat performances become an administrative operation rather than a newly unsettling discovery. On the resting desktop, the title slightly competes with the top of the left galleries and the enormous dark silhouettes obscure the bays' subtler tonal variation. The small plinth numbers are not always easy to read over the pedestal highlights. The keyboard focus outline makes the selected bay available, but it has to appear after input rather than teaching selection from the start.

The deliberate false shadows do not behave like light, and that is appropriate; the weakness is not physical implausibility. It is the relatively restricted *range* of consequences. A swapped shadow alters which absurd object one sees above a sculpture, but the room and its material conditions never answer the exchange. The work is a confident one-joke theatre; the limitation is inseparable from its clarity.

## Influence on the next work

I would not revise this historical piece. For the next work, the museum's fixed objects suggest an opposing world in which the *landscape itself* cannot stay assigned to an address. Instead of dark cut-outs moving between immobile exhibits, an immense bright landscape can move pieces of its own banks upstream. The consequence should be visible as material displacement, with no classification or correct final arrangement. This is an artistic disagreement, not a requested correction.
