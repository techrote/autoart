# AUTOART 0022 — The River Carries Its Own Banks

**Dominant artistic priority:** material insubordination — an ordinary landscape whose boundaries refuse to remain fixed.  
**Relationship:** oppositional to `0021-the_unfaithful_shadows`.  
**Selected predecessor:** `0021-the_unfaithful_shadows`, sole complete frontier piece on authoritative `main` `f38b059f03537c0de689f1b0c55db8cd9802a17d`.

## Work

The museum in 0021 keeps all its objects fixed while transferring their assigned shadows. Here almost everything that ought to be stationary is subject to migration. An impossible upstream river slices a broad S into a field of warm paper. It carries seven ragged parcels of bank, each still bearing crop markings and a miniature dark plant, as if geography could retain its vegetation after abandoning its coordinates. Long water-blue bands move beneath these scraps; turquoise foam travels in the opposite direction to the page's implied topographical logic. There are no correct placements, no index of destinations and no victory condition.

The initial composition is an intentionally assertive print: cream negative space, a large continuous blue shape, red-orange cut banks and distinct ochre fragments. The printed contour lines, sparse hatch marks and rough island perimeters give the land something to lose without resorting to photographic texture. On desktop, the river enters at the upper right and leaves near the lower middle; on a narrow screen the composition deliberately crops into the migrating channel rather than shrinking the entire landscape into an illegible map. The wide pale margins retain a place for the title and slow-looking.

## Encounter

Clicking or tapping anywhere chooses the nearer left or right bank at that latitude and loosens a new piece. It enters from the shore over approximately 2.7 seconds, travels upstream and gradually fades after a bounded twenty-second lifetime. The initial seven scraps also drift upstream indefinitely on repeating, deterministic routes; they do not count toward the interactive cap. Move the pointer to bias the watercourse very slightly at its latitude. Up/down arrows move a visible keyboard latitude marker; Enter releases a piece from alternating banks; Space pauses/resumes all animated time, and R restores the initial geography and seven original islands. A polite live status reports keyboard selection, release, stillness and restoration. Reduced-motion preference starts time stopped until Space is used.

The work uses no audio, simulation of hydrodynamics, coordinates from a service, external images, fonts, framework, server, telemetry, or network. It consists entirely of inline HTML, CSS and Canvas 2D JavaScript. Geography and particle distribution derive from stable integer hashes; render state is bounded to seven continuously recycled initial scraps, eight temporary releases, a fixed number of strokes/specks and one `requestAnimationFrame` chain. Device-pixel ratio is capped at 1.8. Visual motion is slow enough not to strobe.

## Validation

The exact committed HTML candidate's inline JavaScript passed a Node 22 syntax parse. Direct `file://` Chromium navigation was attempted for the predecessor and blocked by managed browser policy. The candidate itself was rendered using Chromium `set_content` with the exact complete HTML text at 1440×900 and 390×844. Actual opening and click-triggered images were captured and inspected at both sizes. Click, ArrowUp, ArrowDown, Enter, Space and R changed the live status as specified, with no page/console exceptions, external requests or dynamic DOM growth; the static element count remained 22. A literal local-file URL remains unqualified in this environment.

## Deliberate limits

It is a moving print, not a fluid solver. A released bank fragment emerges from the nearest shore even if the viewer clicked over dry land; the response is thematic rather than point-local. The established islands wrap their routes rather than conserve physical sediment mass. The plant motifs are intentionally tiny, somewhere between crops and map symbols. Repetition of their movement is a risk: after extended viewing, the river's insubordination may become tranquil rather than surprising. The manuscript does not promise a climax, but each new piece introduces an independent, finite history.
